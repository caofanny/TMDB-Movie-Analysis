Conclusion

Definition of Success: ROI Percentiles
For our analysis, we defined our definition of success based on the 50th percentile/median of the ROI's we calculated. In general, a "good" ROI is considered to be around 10%, but this varies based on the type of investment. In our case, the investment is the production of the movie, in which ROI's are most of the time way greater than 10%. For this reason, we decided to define a successful movie based on the distribution of the data and not a general value of success. Specifically, we defined the 50th percentile as the mark of success for a movie, which in our case, is 134%.

Summary of Findings
Finding 1: Factors that Can Aid Movie Decision Making: Based on our analysis, we found that release date decisions, budget across countries, budget amounts, and genres are factors that we believe can help act as "controllable" variables that can affect the success of a film.

Finding 2: Factors That Can Not Aid Movie Decision Making: We also found that cast size displayed no correlation or relationship to revenue produced (converted to U.S. Dollars).

Our Recommendations for a Successful Movie
Release date (peak periods are summer months): Release a movie in a calmer month like February to get rid of the rapid competition and to stand out.

Global budgets: Research what amount of resources the top competitors are allocating in those markets to remain competitive or focus on regions with lower production costs.

Genre: Produce a movie with a genre that gives safe and consistent results in ROI (animation and romance movies) which although safe, also have potential of achieving extremely successful ROI amounts.

Overall budget: Stick to a smaller budget as we found that as companies increase budget spending, it gets harder to achieve profitability.

Future Work
While our current analysis provides valuable insights into movie profitability factors, several directions could expand this research. Incorporating marketing budget data would provide a more complete picture of total investment and reveal relationships between promotional spending and revenue generation. Extending our dataset to include more recent years from 2017 to 2024 would capture post-pandemic trends and the impact of streaming platforms like Netflix and Disney+ on traditional box office models. Additionally, developing machine learning models could help predict box office revenue based on pre-production factors, enabling stakeholders to make more data-driven investment decisions. Finally, a deeper analysis of international markets and the quantifiable impact of star directors and actors would provide valuable insights into region-specific preferences and the value of talent investments.
