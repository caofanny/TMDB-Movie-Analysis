TMDB Movie Analysis

Introduction to project, including the task and topics (5pts)

Dataset Introduction
The dataset we used is called the TMDB 5000 Movie Dataset, which contains metadata on 4800 movies from Kaggle. This dataset contains information from The Movie Database (TMDb) API. It includes 20 features for data like budget, revenues, genres, popularity, and production company, which covers both categorical (genre) and continuous (budget) data. We will analyze how various features of movie production may contribute to a movie's overall success and returns. Since it is in a CSV file, we will be loading it using Pandas for cleaning and manipulation as part of data visualization.

Definition of Success: ROI Percentiles
For our analysis, we defined our definition of success based on the 50th percentile/median of the ROI's we calculated. In general, a "good" ROI is considered to be around 10%, but this varies based on the type of investment. In our case, the investment is the production of the movie, in which ROI's are most of the time way greater than 10%. For this reason, we decided to define a succesful movie based on the distrubution of the data and not a general value of success. Specifically, we defined the 50th percentile as the mark of success for a movie, which in our case, is 134%.
Summary Of Findings
Finding 1: Factors that Can Aid Movie Decision Making: Based on our analysis, we found that release date decisions, budget accross countries, and genres are factors that we believe can help act as "controllable" variables that can affect the success of a film. When deciding on when a film should be released, filmakers should indentify less crowded months to maximize profit on mid-buget films, peak seasons for movies with large budgets, and overall aim to release movies in oeak seasons to reduce financial risk. Furthermore, when considering what should a film's global and per region budget be, stakeholder's should take in account how leading companies allocate budgets accross different regions to remain competitive. Finally, some genres such as animation and romance prove to be more "safe" than others to invest in, however some show potentials for extreme success despite smaller budgets like horror. Therefore, depending on their goals on their investments and returns films need to largely consider the genre as a factor that they heavily consider regarding that matter.
Finding 2: Factors That Can Not Aid Movie Decision Making: Although variables such as release dates, budgets, and genres proved to be useful variables for decision making some variables observed failed to prove that they were reliable variables to consider for stakeholders. For instance, cast size which was observed in our scatterplot visualization displayed no correlation or relationship to revenue produced (converted to U.S. Dollars).
